how the verdict works

It blends tonight’s forecast Kp (how disturbed Earth’s magnetic field is, 0–9), cloud cover during true darkness, the moon, and live solar wind. From Tasmania, Kp 4 usually means a soft glow on camera; Kp 5+ can be naked-eye; 6+ means drop things and drive. “Go now” only appears when it’s actually dark and reasonably clear right now. It’s a probability, not a promise — the aurora keeps its own schedule.

Last 24 hours, planetary Kp (NOAA). Tasmania usually needs Kp 5+ for a show, Kp 4 for a camera glow on the southern horizon.
what is Kp?

Kp measures how disturbed Earth’s magnetic field is, worldwide, on a 0–9 scale updated every three hours. The further you are from the poles, the higher it needs to be before the aurora reaches you. Tasmania sits well for the southern auroral oval: 4 is worth a glance south, 5 is promising, 6+ is a show. The outlook line is NOAA’s prediction for the biggest bin overlapping tonight’s darkness.

Solar wind

Waiting on the feed…
what do these numbers mean?

The aurora is solar wind hitting Earth’s magnetic field. Two numbers matter most: speed (500+ km/s is fast) and Bz — the wind’s magnetic field tilt. When Bz points south (a negative number), it locks into Earth’s field and pours energy in. Fast wind plus a strongly negative Bz is the recipe; Bt is the field’s overall strength, so a big Bt with Bz south is even better.

how to read the clouds

Each cloud fills to the fraction of sky covered that hour — empty outline means clear, full means overcast. Under about 40% you’re in business; aurora and stars punch through gaps. Over 85% it doesn’t matter what the sun is doing.

Darkness & moon

why these times matter

“Properly dark” is the end of astronomical twilight — the sun 18° below the horizon, no trace of daylight left. That window is when faint things are visible. The moon is the other spotlight: below about 40% illumination, or below the horizon, keeps the sky dark enough for the subtle stuff. A bright aurora doesn’t care about the moon; everything fainter does.

Milky Way core

what’s “the core”?

The bright centre of our galaxy, in Sagittarius — the part the photographs are made of. It needs three things: a dark sky, a low moon, and height above the horizon. From Tasmania it rides high through autumn and winter evenings. The line above is computed for your location: when it’s highest tonight and which way to face.
